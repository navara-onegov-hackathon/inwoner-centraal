# Demo Narrative

## One-line story

When Truus loses her husband, she should not have to become the integration layer between government organisations. Our platform becomes that layer for her.

## Narrative arc

Truus has just lost her husband Cees. Instead of a flood of disconnected letters from RDW, CAK, Toeslagen, SVB, the gemeente, and the waterschap, she gets one place where the government has already assembled what matters.

The platform starts from the death event and delegated authority. It checks which organisations are relevant, retrieves what it can from existing services, and converts fragmented records into one understandable case overview.

For the demo, Truus has chosen the highest service level: handle as much as possible for me.

That means the platform does not wait for her to figure out which letters matter. It proactively:

- identifies what has already been arranged
- flags what still needs attention
- prepares actions where possible
- asks only the questions that truly require her input

The result is a service that feels like one government, even though the underlying systems remain many.

## Core message

This is not an AI chatbot for grief. This is a background intelligence and orchestration layer that reduces burden, assembles fragmented public service information, and lets the government act more like a coordinated whole.

## The problem we show

In the Truus case:

- Cees was registered at a care home
- Truus lives elsewhere
- letters go to different addresses
- some are personal, many are addressed to "de erven van"
- some require no action, others hide urgent obligations
- some arrive within days, others much later

Today, Truus has to interpret that chaos herself.

## The change we demonstrate

With our platform:

- Truus sees a single overview
- the platform explains what is automatic and what is not
- the platform combines digital and paper-originating processes
- the platform asks only minimal follow-up questions
- the platform prepares actions instead of only listing them

## Demo flow

### 1. Start from Truus's perspective

Open on the overview, not on system internals.

The first message to Truus should be:

"We have identified which government matters may apply after Cees's death. We have already arranged what we can and highlighted what still needs your attention."

### 2. Show that the platform has already done work

Demonstrate that the platform has:

- recognized the death context
- linked Truus to Cees through relationship data
- detected that Cees's registered address differs from Truus's home address
- retrieved or inferred relevant obligations and rights

This is the moment where judges should feel the proactive value.

### 3. Surface a prioritised overview

Show a concise overview with categories such as:

- already arranged
- action needed soon
- under review
- may require your confirmation later

Example items:

- SVB overlijdensuitkering: automatic
- CAK invoice: payment pending
- Toeslagen recalculation: review needed
- Waterschap assessment: expected because of peildatum

### 4. Show a guided clarification

Include one short question where the platform genuinely needs Truus.

Good examples:

- confirm preferred correspondence address
- confirm whether the vehicle is still in use
- approve requesting deferral for a payment obligation

This proves the product is not pretending everything can be automated.

### 5. Show action orchestration

After Truus answers, show the platform updating the case:

- a form is prefilled
- an address update is prepared
- a deferral request is queued
- the item changes status

This is the "handelen namens" proof point.

### 6. End on peace of mind

Close with a reduced task list and a clear next timeline.

The emotional outcome matters:

Truus does not need to decode six organisations. She only needs to respond where her judgment is actually needed.

## What makes this compelling to the jury

- It is clearly useful to the bereaved partner.
- It links multiple organisations.
- It is proactive.
- It respects existing government infrastructure instead of replacing everything.
- It uses AI where it adds leverage, but keeps the user experience calm and practical.

## Suggested spoken pitch track

Truus has just lost her husband. Today, that means grief plus an administrative storm: letters to the wrong address, unclear obligations, and no single place to understand what is happening.

Our solution treats that as an orchestration problem. The user tells the platform how much it may handle on their behalf. In this demo, Truus chooses: handle as much as possible.

The platform then checks which organisations are relevant, retrieves data from existing APIs and other channels, normalizes that information, and creates one clear case overview. It shows what is already arranged, what still needs attention, and what it can prepare or do on Truus's behalf.

If information is missing or conflicting, it does not dump complexity on her. It asks one guided question through the interface and then continues.

So the innovation is not an AI chat window. The innovation is that Truus no longer has to act as the integration layer between government organisations.
